# Frontier Lap — Phase 1 source shortlist

November 2024–November 2026, reviewed through November 21, 2026. Trust scores reflect how much weight I’d give each source in its specialty. **5** means detailed evidence with clear limits and disclosures. **4** means strong evidence within a narrower or more subjective scope. **3** means useful with corroboration.

Keep Artificial Analysis as the baseline. Give more weight to dated tests that name the model, harness and access tier. Early access can establish what a reviewer saw. Public availability needs its own evidence. Check for shared benchmark inputs before treating two scores as corroboration. [Artificial Analysis methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking)

- **Simon Willison — 5/5.** Independent developer writing on his blog. Best for API access, pricing, tool use and reproducible coding experiments. Discloses free credits and embargoed previews across labs, and payment for his time at one OpenAI GPT-5 preview. His release archive gives concrete examples and often enough detail to rerun them. [Disclosures](https://simonwillison.net/about/) · [Release archive](https://simonwillison.net/tags/llm-release/)
- **Every — 4/5.** AI publication and product studio led by Dan Shipper. Its Vibe Check reviews and benchmarks cover writing, coding and day-to-day agents. It gets pre-release access and makes AI products. Its 2025 round was co-led by Reid Hoffman, an early OpenAI backer and former board member. Team testing and published rubrics are strong evidence for workloads like Every’s. [Vibe Check example](https://every.to/vibe-check/fable-5-2) · [Engineering benchmark](https://every.to/benchmarks/senior-engineer-benchmark) · [Writing benchmark](https://every.to/benchmarks/writing-benchmark) · [Funding](https://every.to/on-every/so-we-raised-some-money) · [Hoffman’s OpenAI ties](https://openai.com/index/openai-lp/)
- **Theo Browne — 4/5.** T3 Code and T3 Chat creator posting on YouTube and X. Best for coding agents on real projects, harness friction, speed and subscription value. His own tools shape the workflows he tests, and his August 2026 tier-list video discloses sponsors Browserbase and G2i. His cross-lab comparisons show what a developer would notice in daily use. [Site and channels](https://t3.gg/) · [Model tier list](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=06BvFMW8Ng8)
- **Nathan Lambert — 4/5.** Trillium Labs co-founder and former Ai2 post-training lead, writing and podcasting at Interconnects. Best for open weights, Chinese labs, adoption and deployment trade-offs. His work favors open models by design, and he discloses paid advisory work for Arcee AI and Mercor. He tests frontier claims against the economics and uptake of open models. [Bio](https://natolambert.com/contact) · [Disclosures](https://www.interconnects.ai/about) · [Open-model comparison](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-models-recap-more-on-kimi-k3)
- **METR — 5/5.** Nonprofit evaluator publishing research and data on agent autonomy. Best for sustained software-task reliability. It reports accepting free tokens from frontier labs and declining their funding. Some predeployment write-ups require lab approval. Its public time-horizon tracker stopped updating in September 2026, though later research continued. Published methods and raw data make measured results auditable. [Time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) · [Funding](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-14-funding-update/) · [Predeployment disclosure](https://metr.org/blog/2026-06-26-gpt-5-6-sol/) · [November update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)
- **Ethan Mollick — 4/5.** Wharton professor and Generative AI Labs co-director, writing at One Useful Thing and on X. Best for research, presentations, teaching and other knowledge work. He discloses pre-release access in his Fable 5.2 review. Detailed examples reveal where models get stuck, with results tied to the tasks he chose. [Wharton bio](https://mgmt.wharton.upenn.edu/profile/emollick/) · [2024 roundup](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/what-just-happened) · [Fable review](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi)
- **Matt Shumer — 3/5.** HyperWrite CEO and AI investor posting on X and his blog. Best for intensive coding-agent use, steering and cost trade-offs. He reviews with early access, says he usually prefers GPT for his backend work and invests in AI infrastructure and agent tools. His reports give concrete reasons for switching models, drawn from one power user’s workload. [Bio and investments](https://shumer.dev/about) · [GPT-5.6 review](https://somethingbig.ai/gpt-5-6-review)
- **Vals AI — 4/5.** Evaluation company publishing benchmark results and methodology. Best for professional agents in legal, finance, spreadsheet and coding tasks. It sells custom evaluation services and partners with Fireworks. Task-level results reveal where models excel, with some scores reflecting the model and its native agent together. [Vals Index](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/vals_index) · [Methodology](https://www.vals.ai/methodology) · [Fireworks partnership](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/vals-fireworks-partnership)
- **Arena.ai (formerly LMSYS Chatbot Arena) — 3/5.** Crowdsourced comparison platform publishing leaderboards and a research blog. Best for user preferences in chat and writing. It offers commercial lab evaluations. A 2025 study found selective-disclosure bias in historical testing, and Arena later published eligibility rules and a changelog. Treat close preference scores as weak tie-breakers. [Leaderboards](https://arena.ai/leaderboard) · [Commercial evaluations](https://arena.ai/blog/ai-evaluations) · [Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) · [Policy](https://arena.ai/blog/policy)
- **Epoch AI — 4/5.** AI research organization publishing its Benchmarking Hub and Epoch Capabilities Index. Best for difficult math and raw reasoning. OpenAI funded FrontierMath and has exclusive access to a subset. Epoch publishes its methods and runs its own tests, with the funding and access caveat most relevant to FrontierMath comparisons. [Benchmarking Hub](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/about) · [Index methodology](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/methodology) · [Funding and access disclosure](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tiers-1-3-v2)
- **Aider — 4/5.** Open-source coding assistant with a benchmark blog and leaderboard. Best for early-period code editing, instruction following and cost per run. Its maintainers test through their own harness on standalone exercises. Public tasks and per-run settings make comparisons checkable within that setup. [December 2024 launch](https://aider.chat/2024/12/21/polyglot.html) · [Leaderboard](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)

## Recommended top six

1. **Simon Willison** — What shipped, who could use it, and how it behaved in practice.
2. **Every** — Team-tested writing, editing and production coding.
3. **Theo Browne** — Coding-agent performance, harness friction and subscription value.
4. **Nathan Lambert** — Open-weight contenders, especially Chinese labs, and deployment economics.
5. **METR** — Measured reliability on longer software tasks.
6. **Ethan Mollick** — Hands-on judgment for research and other knowledge work.
