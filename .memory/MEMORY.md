# Frontier Lap: project memory

- **index.html must stay one self-contained file.** The owner previews it in a viewer that opens index.html alone, so sibling files such as data.js never load. In Nov 2026 a separate data.js broke the page ("no usable snapshots"). The data now lives in the `<script id="race-data">` block near the top of index.html. Test changes with index.html copied alone into an empty folder.
- **This worktree is `v2-next`** (folder fastest-lab-next, branched from `v2-events`). ../fastest-lab-v2 is `v2-events`, which the owner views live; ../fastest-lab is `main`, tagged v1-time-based, the owner's frozen favourite. Never touch either from here.
- **Editing the race:** change the SNAPSHOTS text table in the DATA block. `gap` is seconds to the car directly ahead. The owner's scale: 1.0 = clear lead, 0.25 = wheel to wheel, 1.7 = big break. Labs and their rebrands go in LABS (`changes: [{from, name, sub, logo}]`), and logo SVG paths go in LOGOS.
- **Event syntax (v2), both optional per snapshot:** `@lab` at the very end of the date line marks whose event the step is (its car glows, its label lights up; the lab must be in LABS and on track in that snapshot or the one before). A line `> caption` right under the date line is the commentary shown in the lower third above the timeline; a second `>` line continues it. Object form: `event: 'lab'`, `caption: '…'`. Mistakes show the yellow warning and a console message that names the snapshot. The table sits in a JS template literal: no backticks, no dollar-curly.
- **Labels are compact on every screen (v2-next):** position and logo on one row, the name under them, then the note (two lines on desktop, three on phones), in the condensed face. Width is capped in layout(): `lw` = 12% of the screen width, between 176 and 204 px on desktop, about 112 px on a phone. The owner found the old 310 px single-row labels too wide and big. They show only position, logo, name and the note. The owner asked for the DRS / FRONTIER LAP / NEW / OUT chips and all label numbers (gap to leader, INT) to go; the big interval numbers on the road stay. A joining lab is announced by its note text; a retiring one gets "Drops out of the fight." `sub` in LABS is no longer shown.
- **Caption height costs field height.** layout() measures the tallest caption at the current width and ends the field above it, so one very long caption makes every car a bit smaller. Under about 110 characters keeps it to two lines on desktop and three on a phone.
- **Knobs:** the TUNING and PLAYBACK blocks just below DATA.
  - `PLAYBACK.spacing`: `'events'` (default since v2) gives one step per event and an evenly spaced timeline with one mark per event; `length` and `transition` are ignored. Since v2-next a step's move lasts as long as its cars need to stay calm: `move` [2.2, 9] seconds (shortest, longest), `topSpeed` 0.24 (fastest any car goes along the road, in field heights per second), `sideSpeed` 0.6 (across it, in road widths per second), then `hold[0]` = 1.3 s at rest. The old `event` knob is gone. The owner found 1.4 to 1.9 s moves much too hectic and accepted a replay of 2 to 2.5 minutes. `'calendar'` and `'uniform'` are the v1 behaviour: `length` 46 s of driving shared by calendar time or evenly, `transition` [1.7, 2.8], calendar timeline.
  - `TUNING.carSize` (0.16) is the car length as a share of the field height; phones are sized by width instead. `sideGap` is now a pair [0.50, 1.00] in car widths: at least the first, up to the second where the screen is wide, which is what makes the road broad on desktop. `noseGap` (0.25 car lengths); `zoom` (1.75) stretches tight fields to fill the height.
  - `TUNING.wobble` (1) is how far the cars drift and surge, `TUNING.pace` (1) how fast. `TUNING.clearance` (0.09 car lengths) is the least road ever left between two cars.
  - `?speed=2` speeds up the replay. GT is the only car style since Nov 2026 (F1 and toy cars, the C key and `?cars=` are gone).
- **How the wobble stays safe:** `elbowRoom()` cuts every car's drift, surge and nose turn pair by pair so that two cars leaning towards each other at once still leave `clearance`. It depends only on where the plan puts the cars, so raising `wobble` or `pace` can never make cars touch; it only changes how lively free cars look. `frontierLap.check()` reports `worst` (least room over the whole timeline with the wobble at its worst) next to `clearance`; `ok: true` needs `worst >= clearance` and `tight: 0`.
- **Timeline marks in events mode:** dates under the rail are placed by priority and only where they fit: the two ends ("NOV 24"), then the year where one begins ("2025"), then the month where a quarter begins ("APR"). A year label right next to an end label is dropped; that is expected.
- **How the cars move (the drive plan):** nothing is simulated while it plays. `buildDrive()` works out, per screen size, a seat for every car at every snapshot and a path for every step between snapshots; a car's position is a pure function of the timeline position. The rule that makes "never more than three abreast" structural: two cars closer than a car length + noseGap along the road are at least a car width + sideGap apart across it, and the road is only three such lanes wide. Steps are planned in the background a few ms at a time (about 1 s in all on a laptop). `frontierLap.check()` in the console forces and measures every step; `ok: true` and `tight: 0` everywhere is the pass; it also lists every step's seconds and peak speeds. A step that can't be driven cleanly is named in a console warning. Knotty steps are retimed (cars move one after the other, WINDOWS) and then take longer; if lane changes have to be quick they come one place at a time. The seats are chosen starting from the grid, so the launch never asks two cars that stay alongside to swap sides.
- **Tight packs no longer shrink the cars (v2-next).** Until then the tightest four-car pack in the data (2025-07-09: four cars within 0.50 s) sized every car on the whole timeline. Now `roadMap()` places each snapshot's cars: to scale, except that a pack of four closer than a car length + noseGap is opened up just enough, and the rest of that picture closes up a little. The same is done for three cars that another car has to get past (an overtake, or a lab driving in or out) when they would be abreast at both ends of the step: nobody can pass three abreast on a three-lane road. The numbers on the road still show the real gaps (`secAt()` maps px back to seconds). `frontierLap.check().opened` lists the affected snapshots; with the events data 2025-07-09 is opened 1.54 times, five others by 4 to 20%. The standings data is the owner's and stays untouched.
- **Logo boxes:** a LOGOS `box` is what gets centred and scaled into the roundel, and the roundel clips. The SpaceX AI box covers only the "XA", so its swoosh tail runs off the disc on purpose.

- **Verify headless** with Playwright `chromium.launch({channel:'chrome'})`; no bundled browsers are installed (install `playwright-core` in a scratch folder). A real-time replay check freezes when the Mac sleeps (lid closed on battery: every timer stretches, it looks like a page hang). The check that always works: replace `requestAnimationFrame` in an init script, step the page's frames yourself at 1/60 s and test after each one. Don't pass `--disable-frame-rate-limit`: it makes the page run at 500 fps and the fps numbers meaningless. The per-frame check reads `frontierLap.cars` (x + wx, by + wy, rot) after each animation frame and tests the rotated car boxes. Checked sizes: 1440x900 and 390x844 (plus 1920x1080, 1024x768, 844x390, 2560x1300, 360x640, 768x1024, 1280x720 for the drive plan).
